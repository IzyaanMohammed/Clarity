## 1.  Introduction  

Chemistry deals with substances and the changes they undergo. Among the many families of substances, **acids, bases and salts** occupy a central place because they are encountered in everyday life, industry and the laboratory. This chapter explores their nature, properties, reactions and the methods used to identify them.  

---  

## 2.  Acids  

### 2.1  Definition  

> **Acid** – A substance that **donates hydrogen ions (H⁺)** in aqueous solution or **produces H⁺ ions** when dissolved in water.  

*According to the Arrhenius definition:* an acid increases the concentration of H⁺ ions in water.  

*According to the Brønsted–Lowry definition:* an acid is a **proton donor**.  

### 2.2  Common Properties  

| Property | Observation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Taste** | Sour (e.g., lemon juice, vinegar) |
| **Effect on litmus** | Turns blue litmus red |
| **Reaction with metals** | Reacts with active metals (Zn, Mg, Fe) to liberate **hydrogen gas** |
| **Reaction with carbonates** | Fizzes with carbonates/ bicarbonates, releasing **CO₂** |
| **Electrical conductivity** | Conducts electricity (electrolyte) because of ions in solution |

### 2.3  Important Formulas  

1. **pH of a solution**  

   $$\text{pH}= -\log_{10}[H^{+}]$$  

2. **Concentration of H⁺ from pH**  

   $$[H^{+}] = 10^{-\text{pH}} \; \text{mol L}^{-1}$$  

3. **Acid‑base neutralisation**  

   $$\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \rightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water}$$  

   In ionic form (for a strong acid and a strong base):  

   $$\underbrace{H^{+}}_{\text{from acid}} + \underbrace{OH^{-}}_{\text{from base}} \longrightarrow H_{2}O$$  

### 2.4  Examples of Common Acids  

| Acid | Formula | Common Use |
|------|---------|------------|
| Hydrochloric acid | HCl | Stomach acid, cleaning metal |
| Sulphuric acid | H₂SO₄ | Battery acid, manufacture of fertilizers |
| Nitric acid | HNO₃ | Explosives, etching |
| Acetic acid | CH₃COOH | Vinegar |
| Carbonic acid | H₂CO₃ | Soft drinks (formed in solution) |

## 3.  Bases  

### 3.1  Definition  

> **Base** – A substance that **accepts hydrogen ions (H⁺)** or **produces hydroxide ions (OH⁻)** in aqueous solution.  

*Arrhenius definition:* a base increases the concentration of OH⁻ ions in water.  

*Brønsted–Lowry definition:* a base is a **proton acceptor**.  

### 3.2  Common Properties  

| Property | Observation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Taste** | Bitter |
| **Feel** | Slippery, soapy feel |
| **Effect on litmus** | Turns red litmus blue |
| **Reaction with acids** | Neutralises acids to give salt + water |
| **Electrical conductivity** | Conducts electricity (electrolyte) |

### 3.3  Important Formulas  

1. **pOH of a solution**  

   $$\text{pOH}= -\log_{10}[OH^{-}]$$  

2. **Relation between pH and pOH**  

   $$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14 \quad (\text{at } 25^{\circ}\text{C})$$  

3. **K\(_\text{b}\) – Base ionisation constant** (for a weak base B)  

   $$B + H_{2}O \rightleftharpoons BH^{+} + OH^{-}$$  

   $$K_{b}= \frac{[BH^{+}][OH^{-}]}{[B]}$$  

### 3.4  Examples of Common Bases  

| Base | Formula | Common Use |
|------|---------|------------|
| Sodium hydroxide | NaOH | Drain cleaner, soap making |
| Potassium hydroxide | KOH | Soft‑ening water |
| Calcium hydroxide | Ca(OH)₂ | Lime water, building material |
| Ammonia solution | NH₃·H₂O | Household cleaner |
| Magnesium hydroxide | Mg(OH)₂ | Antacid (Milk of magnesia) |

## 4.  Salts  

### 4.1  Definition  

> **Salt** – An ionic compound formed **by the neutralisation reaction** of an acid and a base. It contains the **cation of the base** and the **anion of the acid**.  

### 4.2  Formation of Salts  

The general neutralisation equation:  

$$\underbrace{HA}_{\text{acid}} + \underbrace{BOH}_{\text{base}} \longrightarrow \underbrace{BA}_{\text{salt}} + \underbrace{H_{2}O}_{\text{water}}$$  

where **HA** is a generic acid (H⁺ donor) and **BOH** is a generic base (OH⁻ donor).  

#### Example  

Neutralisation of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide:  

$$\boxed{HCl + NaOH \longrightarrow NaCl + H_{2}O}$$  

- **Na⁺** comes from NaOH (base)  
- **Cl⁻** comes from HCl (acid)  

### 4.3  Types of Salts  

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Normal (Simple) Salt** | Both acid and base are strong | NaCl, K₂SO₄ |
| **Acidic Salt** | One replaceable H⁺ remains (from a weak acid) | NaHSO₄, NH₄Cl |
| **Basic Salt** | Contains OH⁻ or a weak base cation | Na₂CO₃, CaCO₃ |
| **Double Salt** | Two different salts crystallise together | Mohr’s salt – (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·6H₂O |
| **Complex Salt** | Contains a complex ion (coordination compound) | K₃[Fe(CN)₆] |

## 5.  Reactions Involving Acids, Bases and Salts  

### 5.1  Neutralisation  

**Equation:**  

$$\boxed{Acid + Base \rightarrow Salt + Water}$$  

**Worked Example 1**  

*Problem:* 25 mL of 0.1 M HCl is mixed with 25 mL of 0.1 M NaOH. Calculate the pH of the resulting solution.  

*Solution:*  

1. Moles of H⁺ = \(0.1 \, \text{M} \times 0.025 \, \text{L}=2.5 \times 10^{-3}\) mol.  
2. Moles of OH⁻ = same (2.5 × 10⁻³ mol).  
3. They neutralise completely → water formed, no excess H⁺ or OH⁻.  
4. The solution is neutral (ignoring auto‑ionisation of water).  

\[
\text{pH}=7
\]

---

### 5.2  Reaction of Acid with Metal  

**General equation:**  

$$\boxed{Metal + Acid \rightarrow Salt + H_{2}\uparrow}$$  

**Worked Example 2**  

*Problem:* Write the balanced equation when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid.  

*Solution:*  

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Zn} + \text{H}_{2}\text{SO}_{4} &\longrightarrow \text{ZnSO}_{4} + \text{H}_{2}\uparrow \\
\end{aligned}
\]

---

### 5.3  Reaction of Acid with Carbonates  

**General equation:**  

$$\boxed{Acid + Carbonate \rightarrow Salt + H_{2}O + CO_{2}\uparrow}$$  

**Worked Example 3**  

*Problem:* 5 g of CaCO₃ is treated with excess HCl. How many millilitres of H₂ gas are produced at STP?  

*Solution:*  

1. Moles of CaCO₃ = \(\frac{5}{100.09}\,\text{mol}=0.0499\) mol.  
2. Reaction: \(\text{CaCO}_{3}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{CaCl}_{2}+H_{2}O+CO_{2}\uparrow\) – no H₂ gas is formed; CO₂ is released.  
   (Hence the answer is **0 mL** of H₂.)  

*Lesson:* Always check the balanced equation before calculating gas volumes.

---

### 5.4  Preparation of Salts  

| Method | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Reaction of acid with base** | NaCl from HCl + NaOH |
| **Reaction of acid with metal oxide** | CuO + 2 H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + 2 H₂O |
| **Reaction of acid with metal carbonate** | Na₂CO₃ + 2 HCl → 2 NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ |
| **Direct combination** | 2 Na + Cl₂ → 2 NaCl (synthesis) |
| **Metathesis (double displacement)** | Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → 2 NaCl + BaSO₄↓ |

## 6.  Indicators – Detecting Acidity or Alkalinity  

| Indicator | Colour in Acidic Solution | Colour in Basic Solution |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Litmus paper | Red | Blue |
| Phenolphthalein | Colourless | Pink |
| Methyl orange | Red | Yellow |
| Bromothymol blue | Yellow | Blue |

*The colour change occurs because the indicator itself is a weak acid or base that dissociates differently at different pH.*

## 7.  Important Formulas (Summary)  

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{pH} &= -\log[H^{+}] \\
\text{pOH} &= -\log[OH^{-}] \\
\text{pH} + \text{pOH} &= 14 \quad (25^{\circ}\text{C})\\[4pt]
K_{a} &= \frac{[H^{+}][A^{-}]}{[HA]} \quad (\text{acid dissociation constant})\\
K_{b} &= \frac{[BH^{+}][OH^{-}]}{[B]} \quad (\text{base ionisation constant})\\[4pt]
\text{Neutralisation: } & HA + BOH \rightarrow BA + H_{2}O\\[4pt]
\text{Acid + Metal: } & \text{Metal} + 2HA \rightarrow \text{MetalA}_{2} + H_{2}\uparrow\\
\text{Acid + Carbonate: } & \text{Acid} + \text{MCO}_{3} \rightarrow \text{M}^{2+}\text{A}_{2} + H_{2}O + CO_{2}\uparrow
\end{aligned}
\]

## 8.  Worked Numerical Problems  

### Problem 1  

*Find the pH of a solution prepared by dissolving 0.5 g of NaOH (M.W. = 40 g mol⁻¹) in 250 mL of water.*  

**Solution**  

1. Moles of NaOH = \(\frac{0.5}{40}=0.0125\) mol.  
2. Concentration of OH⁻ = \(\frac{0.0125}{0.250}=0.050\) M.  
3. \(\text{pOH}= -\log(0.050)=1.30\).  
4. \(\text{pH}=14-1.30=12.70\).  

*The solution is strongly basic.*

---

### Problem 2  

*20 mL of 0.2 M H₂SO₄ is mixed with 30 mL of 0.1 M NaOH. Calculate the pH of the resulting solution.*  

**Solution**  

1. Moles of \(H_{2}SO_{4}\) (first ionisation gives 2 H⁺, but in dilute solution we treat both):  

   \(n_{H^{+}} = 2 \times 0.2 \times 0.020 = 0.008\) mol.  

2. Moles of NaOH = \(0.1 \times 0.030 = 0.003\) mol.  

3. Excess \(H^{+}\) = \(0.008 - 0.003 = 0.005\) mol.  

4. Total volume = 0.020 L + 0.030 L = 0.050 L.  

5. \([H^{+}] = \frac{0.005}{0.050}=0.10\) M.  

6. \(\text{pH}= -\log(0.10)=1.00\).  

*The mixture remains strongly acidic.*

---

### Problem 3  

*Write the net ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous solutions of barium chloride and potassium sulphate.*  

**Solution**  

Overall:  

\[
\text{BaCl}_{2(aq)} + \text{K}_{2}\text{SO}_{4(aq)} \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_{4(s)} \downarrow + 2 \, \text{KCl}_{(aq)}
\]

Spectator ions: \(K^{+}\) and \(Cl^{-}\).  

**Net ionic equation:**  

\[
\boxed{\text{Ba}^{2+}_{(aq)} + \text{SO}_{4}^{2-}_{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_{4(s)}\downarrow}
\]

## 9.  Key Points (Summary)  

1. **Acids** are proton donors; they increase \([H^{+}]\) in water, turn blue litmus red and taste sour.  
2. **Bases** are proton acceptors; they increase \([OH^{-}]\) in water, turn red litmus blue and feel slippery.  
3. **pH** quantifies acidity: \(\text{pH} = -\log[H^{+}]\); \(\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14\) at 25 °C.  
4. **Neutralisation** of an acid and a base yields a **salt** and **water**.  
5. **Salts** are ionic compounds formed from the cation of a base and the anion of an acid; they may be neutral, acidic or basic.  
6. Common reactions:  
   - Acid + Metal → Salt + H₂↑  
   - Acid + Carbonate → Salt + H₂O + CO₂↑  
   - Acid + Base → Salt + H₂O (neutralisation)  
7. **Indicators** help identify the nature of a solution by colour change.  
8. Important constants: \(K_{a}\) for acids, \(K_{b}\) for bases; they describe the degree of ionisation.
