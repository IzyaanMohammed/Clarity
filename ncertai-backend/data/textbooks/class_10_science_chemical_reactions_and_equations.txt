**Chapter – Chemical Reactions and Equations**

### 1.  What is a Chemical Reaction?  

A **chemical reaction** is a process in which one or more substances (reactants) undergo a chemical change to form new substances (products) having different chemical composition and properties.  

**Key features of a chemical reaction**  

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Formation of new substances** | Reactants → Products (different composition). |
| **Energy change** | Usually accompanied by release (exothermic) or absorption (endothermic) of energy. |
| **Reversibility** | Some reactions are reversible; equilibrium may be established. |
| **Conservation of mass** | Total mass of reactants = total mass of products (Law of Conservation of Mass). |

---

### 2.  Types of Chemical Reactions  

| Type of Reaction | General Form | Typical Example |
|------------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Combination (Synthesis)** | A + B → AB | 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O |
| **Decomposition** | AB → A + B | 2 HgO → 2 Hg + O₂ |
| **Displacement (Single‑Replacement)** | A + BC → AC + B | Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu |
| **Double‑Displacement (Metathesis)** | AB + CD → AD + CB | AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃ |
| **Redox (Oxidation–Reduction)** | Electron transfer occurs | Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃ |

*Only the first four types are explicitly required for Class 10 NCERT syllabus.*

### 3.  Balancing Chemical Equations  

Balancing ensures that the **Law of Conservation of Mass** is obeyed.  

#### 3.1 Steps to Balance an Equation  

1. **Write the correct formulas** of all reactants and products.  
2. **List the number of atoms** of each element on both sides.  
3. **Adjust coefficients** (whole numbers placed before formulas) to equalise the number of atoms of each element.  
4. **Check** that the total number of atoms of every element is the same on both sides.  

#### 3.2 Example – Balancing a Combustion Reaction  

Unbalanced:  C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O  

| Element | Reactants | Products |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| C       | 2         | 1 (in CO₂) |
| H       | 6         | 2 (in H₂O) |
| O       | 2         | 2 (in CO₂) + 1 (in H₂O) = 3 |

**Balancing steps**  

- Put coefficient **2** before CO₂ → C: 2 on each side.  
- Put coefficient **3** before H₂O → H: 6 on each side.  
- Now O atoms on product side = (2 × 2) + (3 × 1) = 7.  
- Place coefficient **7/2** before O₂ → O: (7/2) × 2 = 7.  

To avoid fractions, multiply whole equation by 2:  

\[
\boxed{2\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + 7\,\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 4\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}
}
\]

### 4.  Writing Chemical Equations  

#### 4.1 State Symbols  

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| (s) | Solid |
| (l) | Liquid |
| (g) | Gas |
| (aq) | Aqueous solution (solute dissolved in water) |

The state symbol is written in brackets immediately after the formula.  

#### 4.2 Example – Reaction of Sodium with Hydrochloric Acid  

\[
\boxed{2\,\text{Na (s)} + 2\,\text{HCl (aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\text{NaCl (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}}
\]

### 5.  Important Reactions from the Syllabus  

| Reaction | Balanced Equation | Type |
|----------|-------------------|------|
| **Combustion of methane** | CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O | Combination |
| **Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate** | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ | Decomposition |
| **Reaction of zinc with copper sulphate** | Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu | Single‑replacement |
| **Acid‑base neutralisation (HCl + NaOH)** | HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O | Double‑displacement |
| **Precipitation reaction (AgNO₃ + NaCl)** | AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃ | Double‑displacement |

*The arrow “↓” denotes a precipitate.*

### 6.  Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions  

#### 6.1 Exothermic and Endothermic  

- **Exothermic reaction** – energy is released to the surroundings (temperature of surroundings rises).  
  \[
  \text{Reactants} \rightarrow \text{Products} + \text{heat}
  \]

- **Endothermic reaction** – energy is absorbed from the surroundings (temperature of surroundings falls).  
  \[
  \text{Reactants} + \text{heat} \rightarrow \text{Products}
  \]

#### 6.2 Practical Indicators  

| Observation | Reaction Type |
|-------------|---------------|
| Rise in temperature, flame, hot plate | Exothermic |
| Cooling of the reaction mixture, need for heating | Endothermic |

### 7.  Laws Governing Chemical Reactions  

| Law | Statement |
|-----|------------|
| **Law of Conservation of Mass** | Mass of reactants = mass of products in a chemical reaction. |
| **Law of Constant Composition** | A given chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass. |
| **Law of Multiple Proportions** | When two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other are in simple whole‑number ratios. |

   \]

---

### 9.  Summary of Key Points  

- A chemical reaction converts reactants into products with different chemical identities.  
- Reactions are classified as combination, decomposition, single‑replacement, double‑replacement, and redox.  
- Balancing equations respects the conservation of mass; only coefficients are altered, never subscripts.  
- State symbols (s, l, g, aq) indicate the physical state of each species.  
- Energy changes are classified as exothermic (heat released) or endothermic (heat absorbed).  
- Fundamental laws – conservation of mass, constant composition, and multiple proportions – govern all chemical reactions.
